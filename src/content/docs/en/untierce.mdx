| **Customizable** | Yes (number of races) |

## How it works

Each player gets **1,000 play chips** when they join. In every race, six ducks line up, each with its own odds (from ×2 for the favourite to ×8 for the outsider). Players bet on the duck they think will win. If their duck wins, they earn their stake multiplied by the odds. At the end of the night, the biggest stack wins.

<Callout type="info">
The chips are play chips: they have no value and cannot be exchanged for anything. It is a game for fun, not gambling.
</Callout>

## Before you start

On the game's home screen, pick the **number of races**: 3 (default), 5, 8, 12, 20 or 30. You can also set it from the [remote](/docs/screen-controls) on your phone. Then click **Start the night** (it appears as soon as one player has joined).

| Number of races | Approximate duration |
|---|---|
| 3 | 5 to 6 minutes |
| 5 | 10 minutes |
| 8 | 15 minutes |
| 12 | 20 to 25 minutes |

<Callout type="tip">
A QR code stays in a corner of the TV throughout the game: latecomers can join at any time and also get 1,000 chips.
</Callout>

## The game

### A race, step by step

1. **The paddock**: the TV introduces the six ducks and their odds. Betting is already open on the phones
2. **Betting**: each player picks a duck, then a stake, and confirms their ticket. They have 20 seconds and can change their mind until betting closes
3. **No more bets!**: the TV reveals who bet on which duck
4. **The race**: about 45 seconds of racing with live commentary and twists. Players can cheer their duck on by tapping their phone (it shows on the TV, without changing the result)
5. **The finish**: the TV shows the top three, everyone's winnings and the ranking

### Stakes

- One bet per race, on a single duck: the one that will win
- Minimum stake: 10 chips. The all-in button bets everything
- A player with no chips left can place a free bet: if their duck wins, the house gives them 200 chips to get back in the game

### End of the game

The podium reveals the top three, then the rest of the ranking, with two trophies: the biggest win of the night and the duck of the night. Starting a new game puts everyone back to 1,000 chips.

## Your controls during the game

These buttons are **on the TV**: keep the computer within reach.

- **Close bets**: no need to wait for the countdown to end
- **Race 2/3** (the button shows the number of the next race): after each finish, you start it yourself. Take the time to comment on the results. After the last race, the button becomes **See the podium**
- **End now**: go straight to the podium
- **House chips** (the 🪙 button): give 250, 500 or 1,000 chips to one player (each phone shows its number) or to the whole room

From the remote on your phone, you can start, pause, stop and change the number of races.

## Hosting tips

**At the start:**
"Welcome to the racetrack! Tonight, the ducks are racing. You all have 1,000 chips: pick your duck, pick your stake, and confirm your ticket. The favourite pays little, the outsider pays big. Whoever has the biggest stack at the end wins the night!"

**During the game:**
- Introduce the ducks like a real commentator: each one has a name and a personality
- When the bets are in, comment on the room's choices: "Everyone's on the favourite, nobody believes in the outsider…"
- During the race, get the room cheering out loud
- A player has run dry? Give them chips with the 🪙 button: it always goes down well

**For a full evening:**
Pick 8 or 12 races and announce the ranking every 3 or 4 races to keep attention high.

<Callout type="tip">
Untiercé works very well as a running thread, one race between two other games. In that case pick 20 or 30 races and switch games after a finish: everyone gets their chips back when you return, for 3 hours. Note that the podium ends the night: chips then restart at 1,000.
</Callout>
